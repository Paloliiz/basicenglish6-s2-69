Who's in a Hurry?

If dining at an expensive restaurant is out of your budget, fast-casual dining is a great alternative. It may not be as luxurious as a traditional sit-down restaurant, but it provides a better standard of food than typical fast-food chains, meaning you can enjoy a meal without a huge impact on your finances. This dining concept began in the early 1990s but really gained popularity in the 2000s. It continues to grow, even during times of economic recession, because it meets the demands of the modern consumer: healthy options, moderate prices, and a trendy atmosphere.

Another major trend affecting our spending habits is fast fashion. Similar to fast-casual food, this industry targets consumers who want stylish clothes but prefer not to pay extremely high prices. While high-fashion brands create unique and exclusive trends, fast-fashion companies take inspiration from these exclusive designs and produce affordable versions for the general public.

Buying fast fashion might seem like a great deal at first glance, but it often isn't when you consider the "cost-per-wear." This concept measures the true value of an item over time. For instance, purchasing $30 shoes that you only wear once means your cost-per-wear is $30. However, if you invest $150 in a quality pair of shoes and wear them daily for a year, the cost-per-wear drops to less than $1.

The reality is that fast-fashion items usually have a high cost-per-wear. Since they are manufactured using cheaper materials, they tend to wear out quickly. If you have to constantly replace damaged clothes, you aren't actually saving money. Furthermore, low prices encourage people to buy outfits for one-off occasions, which also leads to poor cost-per-wear. Ultimately, calculating this cost is an excellent way to evaluate if your purchases are genuinely economical.

Enjoying trendy food and fashion is fun, but it shouldn't empty your bank account. Unless you are a famous movie star or somehow find a way to grow money on trees, you need to be mindful of your spending habits if you truly want to save money.
